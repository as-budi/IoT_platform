Other apps can let you sign in with your IoT Platform account, for example a web MQTT client. They never see your password.

Signing in to another app

  1. In the other app, choose to sign in with IoT Platform. You are sent to this platform (log in if needed, or sign up first).
  2. The page Allow …? lists what the app asks for. Click Allow to continue or Cancel to refuse.
PermissionWhat the app gets
Sign you inproof that it is you
Profile, emailyour username and name; your email address
Devicesyour devices and the devices shared with you, including the access tokens of devices you can edit (anyone with a token can act as that device)
MQTT sandboxyour sandbox account, including its password

Managing connected apps

My profile → Connected apps → Manage lists the apps you allowed, when, and when they were last used. Revoke access stops an app at once; it has to ask you again next time.

For app developers

Apps are registered by the platform administrator. Registered apps use OAuth 2.0 with OpenID Connect (authorization code with PKCE) and call the API with the access token they receive:

curl -H "Authorization: Bearer <access_token>" {{ http.base_url }}/api/v2/me/devices
RequestReturnsScope
GET /api/v2/methe signed-in user (email only with the email scope)profile
GET /api/v2/me/devicesbroker details, devices with role, status, firmware, MAC address, variables, and token and topics where alloweddevices:read
GET /api/v2/me/sandboxthe user's MQTT sandbox accountmqtt:sandbox

Answers: 200 success, 401 invalid_token (missing, wrong, expired or revoked token, or inactive account), 403 insufficient_scope (the user did not allow that permission). A browser login session does not work on /api/v2/. The OpenID Connect discovery document is at {{ http.base_url }}/o/.well-known/openid-configuration.
